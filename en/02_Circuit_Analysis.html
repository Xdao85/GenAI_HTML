Phasors, Resonance and RLC Transients

An inductor and a capacitor trade stored energy back and forth while the resistor dissipates it. Here one series RLC circuit is studied twice: in AC steady state, where impedance and the phasor diagram explain resonance, and after a DC step, where an RK4 simulation is checked against the exact solution.

Level E · CIR-E

Phasors and Impedance of R, L and C

In AC steady state every signal oscillates at the source frequency, so only amplitude and phase matter: $v(t)=V_m\cos(\omega t+\phi)$ becomes the phasor $\mathbf{V}=V_m e^{j\phi}$. Differentiation becomes multiplication by $j\omega$, giving each element a complex Ohm's law $\mathbf{V}=Z\mathbf{I}$; $Z$ fixes both the amplitude ratio and the phase shift.

Resistor

$$Z_R=R$$

Voltage and current in phase.

Inductor

$$Z_L=j\omega L$$

Voltage leads current by $90^\circ$; $|Z_L|$ rises with $\omega$.

Capacitor

$$Z_C=\frac{1}{j\omega C}$$

Voltage lags current by $90^\circ$; $|Z_C|$ falls with $\omega$.

Series combination

$$Z=R+j\left(\omega L-\frac{1}{\omega C}\right),\qquad |Z|=\sqrt{R^2+X^2},\quad \varphi=\arctan\frac{X}{R}$$

Worked example: $R=30\,\Omega$, $\omega L=80\,\Omega$, $1/(\omega C)=40\,\Omega$ gives $Z=30+j40\,\Omega$, $|Z|=50\,\Omega$, $\varphi=53.1^\circ$. Net $X$ is inductive, so current lags voltage.

Level M · CIR-M

Series RLC in AC Steady State

The same current flows through every element, so $\mathbf{I}$ is the reference phasor; the element voltages, added head to tail, form $\mathbf{V}_s$. Sweep $f$ through $f_0$: $\mathbf{V}_L$ and $\mathbf{V}_C$ cancel, $|Z|$ drops to $R$ and the current peaks. Then lower $R$: the dip sharpens, $Q$ rises and $|\mathbf{V}_C|$ can greatly exceed $V_s$: the basis of tuned circuits.

Phasor calculation ($V_s=10$ V)

$X_L=\omega L$, $X_C=1/\omega C$
$Z=R+jX$
$|Z|$, $\varphi$
$|\mathbf{I}|=V_s/|Z|$
$|\mathbf{V}_R|,|\mathbf{V}_L|,|\mathbf{V}_C|$
$f_0=1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$
$Q=\frac{1}{R}\sqrt{L/C}$

Level C · CIR-C

RLC Step Transient

At $t=0$ a DC source $V_s$ is switched onto the series RLC circuit. Since $v_C$ and $i$ cannot jump, the circuit needs time to settle. The state equations $\dot v_C=i/C$, $\dot i=(V_s-Ri-v_C)/L$ are integrated with RK4 and compared with the analytical response. Lower $R$ to pass from over-damped (slow, no overshoot) through critically damped to under-damped (overshoot and ringing, as in switching transients); reduce $N$ to see RK4 lose accuracy.

MATLAB reference script
R = 20; L = 10e-3; C = 10e-6; Vs = 10; V0 = 0;
A = [0 1/C; -1/L -R/L];  B = [0; Vs/L];
f = @(t,x) A*x + B;
T = 10/max(R/(2*L), 1/sqrt(L*C));
[t,x] = ode45(f, linspace(0,T,1001), [V0; 0], odeset('RelTol',1e-10));
plot(t*1e3, x(:,1)); xlabel('t (ms)'); ylabel('v_C (V)')
